What's happening at this voltage

Why low and high frequency split apart

In inversion, the extra charge at the surface comes from minority carriers (electrons in p-type silicon). Silicon can only make them slowly, by thermal generation. If the AC test signal is slow, they keep up and pile up right under the insulator, so the capacitance climbs back to Cox. At 100 kHz to 1 MHz, the usual probe-station frequency, they can't keep up. Only the edge of the depletion region responds, so the curve stays flat at Cmin.

AccumulationThe gate pulls majority carriers to the surface. The semiconductor acts like a metal plate touching the insulator, so you measure just the insulator: C = Cox.
DepletionThe gate pushes majority carriers away, leaving behind fixed, charged dopant ions. That empty depletion region acts like a second insulator in series, and it gets thicker as VG grows, so C drops.
InversionPast the threshold voltage VT, a thin layer of minority carriers forms at the surface. This is the channel that switches a MOSFET on. The depletion region stops growing at Wmax.

What is an MIS capacitor?

An MIS (metal–insulator–semiconductor) capacitor is a stack of three layers: a metal gate, a thin insulator, and a semiconductor wafer. When the insulator is silicon dioxide it is usually called a MOS capacitor. It is the same stack that sits at the heart of every MOSFET, the transistor in almost every chip, just without the source and drain.

In an ordinary capacitor, both plates are metal and the capacitance is fixed. Here one "plate" is a semiconductor, and the gate voltage changes where its charge sits. Sometimes the charge sits right against the insulator, and sometimes it is pushed deeper into the silicon. So the capacitance depends on voltage, and its shape tells you a lot about the device.

That's why the C–V measurement is the standard first test after fabricating one. With a probe on the gate pad and the wafer chuck as the back contact, a single sweep gives you the insulator thickness, the substrate doping, and how much unwanted charge got trapped in the insulator during processing.
