How A1 and A2 make Smith-Waterman faster

Comparing two proteins means filling a grid of scores. Here, your protein is compared with three database proteins, so there are three grids. The baseline, A1 and A2 fill exactly the same squares and get exactly the same scores. They differ only in how many squares they fill per step.

What this shows, at real size

All three methods give identical scores. The difference is the number of steps. In the real search (one 291-letter query against 1,000 proteins), the baseline takes about 66.8 million steps, one per square. A2 takes 4,656 steps: 291 rows × 16 batches of 64 proteins. That count comes from verify_yourself.py. Each A2 step does more work, so the measured speed-up is 45×, not the full drop in steps.

A1 is based on SWIPE (Rognes, 2011): one square for many database proteins at once. A2 adds the scan-based reformulation (Khajeh-Saeed, Poole & Perot, 2010): a whole row at once.
